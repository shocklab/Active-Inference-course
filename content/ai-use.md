## How these materials were made

This course was developed by Jonathan Shock, Department of Mathematics & Applied
Mathematics, University of Cape Town, with substantial assistance from Claude
(Anthropic), with revisions using Codex (OpenAI) and Gemini (Google). AI assistance extended
to the mathematical content and code, as well as the wording and presentation.

Jonathan supplied his own notes and examples, set the audience and teaching
approach, and directed revisions through questions and feedback. He is the named
author and is responsible for the published course. His own source notes should
not be assumed to be AI-generated.

## What AI was used for

- Planning the course structure, lessons and exercises.
- Drafting and revising explanations, worked derivations and solutions.
- Developing examples, interactive figures, Python notebooks and the website.
- Identifying sources and helping to check citations and numerical claims.
- Reviewing drafts for mathematical errors, undefined notation, missing
  intermediate steps and unclear writing.

Claude assisted with the initial development. Codex subsequently reviewed and
revised the explanations, assumptions, notation and code, and added further
interactive examples. Gemini wrote the first draft of Week 4 and an audit of
Weeks 1 to 3; Claude and Codex then checked both and revised Week 4. These
contributions are substantial; they are not limited to proofreading.

## Review and verification

Jonathan's feedback has shaped the course and identified errors and gaps in its
explanations. AI reviewers have also been used. An AI review is not a human review,
and this disclosure does not claim that every line has been independently checked
by Jonathan or another subject expert.

Checks include recomputing numerical examples, executing notebooks, inspecting
rendered pages and interactive controls, and verifying reference metadata against
Crossref. Where practical, different numerical methods are compared. These checks
can catch particular failures; they do not establish that every argument is
correct or every explanation is clear. A DOI resolving correctly also does not
establish that its paper supports the claim made beside the citation.

AI-assisted drafting and review have produced errors in this course, including
incorrect numerical claims, notation conflicts and explanations that skipped
necessary steps. Revisions have addressed identified problems, but further errors
may remain. The notebooks have been run on Google Colab, on a runtime the size of
the free tier, and every check in them passes there. Iteration counts can differ
slightly from one machine to another, so the Week&nbsp;4 NumPy notebook prints
them beside the lesson's without checking them.
