Problems

Exercise 3.5.1The interneuron rule

In the circuit of Lesson 2 that learns $\Sigma_u$ with an extra unit, from Bogacz (2017), in his section on learning the variance of a single error unit, the error unit $\varepsilon$ and the inhibitory unit $e$ follow

$$\dot\varepsilon \;=\; u - g(d) - e, \qquad \dot e \;=\; \Sigma_u\,\varepsilon - e ,$$

with $d$ held fixed.

(a) Find the fixed point and show that it is stable.

(b) At the fixed point, show that the update $\Delta\Sigma_u = \beta(\varepsilon e - 1)$ uses only the activities of the two units the connection of strength $\Sigma_u$ joins, and that its drift vanishes where $\mathbb E_{P^*}[(u - g(d))^2] = \Sigma_u$.

(c) Show that it equals $2\Sigma_u$ times the gradient Lesson 2 derived for $\Sigma_u$, and say what the factor changes.

Solution

(a) Setting both derivatives to zero: the second gives $e = \Sigma_u\varepsilon$, and the first then gives $u - g(d) = \Sigma_u\varepsilon$. So $\varepsilon = (u - g(d))/\Sigma_u$, the precision-weighted error, and $e = u - g(d)$, the raw residual. The system is linear with matrix

$$\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ \Sigma_u & -1 \end{pmatrix},$$

whose trace is $-1$ and determinant $\Sigma_u > 0$. Two eigenvalues with negative sum and positive product have negative real parts, so the fixed point is stable.

(b) The connection of strength $\Sigma_u$ runs from $\varepsilon$ to $e$, and $\varepsilon e - 1$ is built from those two activities. At the fixed point $\varepsilon e = (u - g(d))^2/\Sigma_u$, so the drift vanishes where $\mathbb E_{P^*}[(u - g(d))^2] = \Sigma_u$.

(c) The gradient is $\tfrac12\big((u - g)^2/\Sigma_u^2 - 1/\Sigma_u\big)$. Multiplying by $2\Sigma_u$ gives $(u - g)^2/\Sigma_u - 1 = \varepsilon e - 1$. The factor is positive, so the direction of every step and the stationary point are unchanged; it rescales the step, making it smaller when $\Sigma_u$ is small, which is where Lesson 2's warning showed the plain gradient misbehaving.

Exercise 3.5.2The sensory variance collapses too †

Use the linear model of Lesson 4, but now let the animal know the prior variance, $\Sigma_p = \Sigma_p^{*}$, and learn the sensory variance by the point rule. Write $\Sigma_u^{*}$ for the world's value. Find the stationary points and the condition for them to exist.

Solution

The residual at the mode is

$$u - \hat d \;=\; (u - d_p) - \frac{\Sigma_p}{\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u}(u - d_p) \;=\; \frac{\Sigma_u}{\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u}\,(u - d_p),$$

so its average square over the process is $\Sigma_u^2(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u^{*})/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)^2$. The point rule's drift vanishes where this equals $\Sigma_u$:

$$\Sigma_u(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u^{*}) \;=\; (\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)^2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Sigma_u^2 + (\Sigma_p - \Sigma_u^{*})\,\Sigma_u + \Sigma_p^2 \;=\; 0 .$$

This is Lesson 4's quadratic with the two variances exchanged, so the same three cases follow. Real positive roots need $\Sigma_u^{*} \geq 3\Sigma_p$; at equality the two coincide at $\Sigma_u = \Sigma_p$, a root that attracts only from above, and above equality the larger root attracts and the smaller separates collapse from survival. A channel whose noise is less than three times the prior's spread has its variance learned all the way to zero: the more the prior can absorb, the less noise the rule sees. The model is symmetric in the two variances because $d$ and $u - d$ enter it in the same way.

Exercise 3.5.3Adding the missing term

In the linear model of Lesson 4, the animal climbs $\hat\ell + \tfrac12\ln(2\pi\Sigma_{\text{post}})$ in $\Sigma_p$ instead of $\hat\ell$. Show that the drift now vanishes at $\Sigma_p = \Sigma_p^{*}$, and say why this does not follow from the point rule plus a correction that would be available in Week 2's model.

Solution

From Lesson 4, the derivative of $\mathbb E_{P^*}[\hat\ell]$ is $(\Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u)/\big(2(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)^2\big) - 1/(2\Sigma_p)$. With $\ln\Sigma_{\text{post}} = \ln\Sigma_p + \ln\Sigma_u - \ln(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$, the derivative of $\tfrac12\ln(2\pi\Sigma_{\text{post}})$ is $1/(2\Sigma_p) - 1/\big(2(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)\big)$. The $1/(2\Sigma_p)$ terms cancel, leaving

$$\frac{\Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u}{2(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)^2} - \frac{1}{2(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)},$$

which vanishes at $\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u = \Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u$, that is at the truth.

In the linear model the correction is exact because the posterior is Gaussian and its width does not depend on $u$. In Week 2's model the posterior is skewed and its width changes from sound to sound, so $\tfrac12\ln(2\pi\Sigma_{\text{post}})$ with a Gaussian width read from the curvature at the mode is an approximation. Week 4 makes that approximation explicit as the Laplace approximation and finds where it fails.

Exercise 3.5.4A hyperprior delays the collapse

In the linear model with $\Sigma_p^{*} < 3\Sigma_u$, add to the point rule's batch objective over $N$ trials the inverse-gamma hyperprior of Lesson 4 with $n_0$ and $\Sigma_0$.

(a) Show that the objective is now bounded above and has a maximum at a positive $\Sigma_p$ for every $N$.

(b) Show that as $N \to \infty$ with $n_0$ fixed, the maximising $\Sigma_p$ tends to zero. What does that say about a hyperprior with a fixed number of pseudo-trials, and what would change if the hyperprior were shared across a fixed window of trials however long the learner runs?

Solution

(a) Write the expected point-rule objective per trial, the expected settled log joint, as $\bar\ell(\Sigma_p) = -(\Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u)/\big(2(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)\big) - \tfrac12\ln\Sigma_p$ up to constants, from Lesson 4. This is the batch objective in expectation, and (b) works with it. A realised batch of $N$ trials has the mean squared deviation of its own observations from $d_p$, call it $B_N$, in place of $\Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u$; the argument of this part uses only that the number is positive, so it holds for a realised batch too. The batch objective is $N\bar\ell(\Sigma_p) - (n_0/2 + 1)\ln\Sigma_p - n_0\Sigma_0/(2\Sigma_p)$. As $\Sigma_p \to 0$, the last term goes to $-\infty$ like $1/\Sigma_p$, faster than the $+\ln$ terms grow, and as $\Sigma_p \to \infty$ the $-\ln\Sigma_p$ terms go to $-\infty$. A continuous function on $(0, \infty)$ that tends to $-\infty$ at both ends has a maximum.

(b) Write $x = \Sigma_p$, $S = \Sigma_p^{*}$, $s = \Sigma_u$ and $A = n_0\Sigma_0$. Setting the derivative of the batch objective to zero and multiplying by $2x^2$,

$$A \;-\; (n_0 + 2)\,x \;-\; N x\left[1 - \frac{(S + s)\,x}{(x + s)^2}\right] \;=\; 0 .$$

The bracket is $1 - (S+s)x/(x+s)^2$. Since $x/(x+s)^2$ is largest at $x = s$, where it is $1/(4s)$, the bracket is at least $1 - (S+s)/(4s) = (3s - S)/(4s)$, which is positive when $S < 3s$. So at every stationary point $N x\,(3s - S)/(4s) \leq A$, that is

$$x \;\leq\; \frac{4sA}{(3s - S)\,N} ,$$

and every stationary point, the maximiser included, tends to zero as $N \to \infty$. For a realised batch the same steps need $B_N < 4s$ in place of $S + s < 4s$, and a finite batch need not meet that even when $S < 3s$, since its mean squared deviation can exceed its expectation. With independent trials $B_N \to S + s$ as $N \to \infty$ by the law of large numbers, so for large enough $N$ the bound applies to the realised batch as well. Near zero the bracket tends to one, so the maximiser is $x \approx A/(N + n_0 + 2) \approx n_0\Sigma_0/N$ for large $N$.

A hyperprior worth a fixed number of trials is outweighed by the data in the end, and if the rule's own drift is negative everywhere, the data push the variance to zero. Sharing the hyperprior across a fixed window of $N_w$ trials, whatever the length of the run, keeps its weight relative to each trial's data fixed. The objective over that window then has a positive maximiser, by the argument of (a): the term $-n_0\Sigma_0/(2\Sigma_p)$ still sends it to $-\infty$ as $\Sigma_p \to 0$. The bound above, with $N_w$ in place of $N$, is an upper bound on that maximiser, of order $n_0\Sigma_0/N_w$; it does not show that the maximiser is positive, and neither statement shows that an online learner converges to it. Choosing $N_w$ is a modelling decision. The learning rate suggests an order of scale, since by Lesson 1 an online learner relaxes over about $1/\big(\beta|\varphi'|\big)$ trials, but it does not fix one. Either way, the hyperprior bounds the objective without removing the bias of the point rule.

Exercise 3.5.5Choosing a learning rate

In the linear running average of Lesson 1 with $\kappa = \beta/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$, the prior location's memory has half-life $\ln 2/(-\ln(1 - \kappa))$ trials and its fluctuation about the target has variance $\kappa\,(\Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u)/(2 - \kappa)$, where $\Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u$ is the variance of $u$.

(a) For small $\kappa$, by what factor must $\beta$ shrink to halve the standard deviation of the fluctuation, and what happens to the half-life?

(b) The world's location jumps once to a new value. Describe the trade-off between tracking the jump and settling accurately, and say what quantity a learner would need in order to choose $\beta$ well.

Solution

(a) For small $\kappa$ the variance is about $\kappa(\Sigma_p^{*} + \Sigma_u)/2$, so the standard deviation scales as $\sqrt\kappa$, hence as $\sqrt\beta$. Halving it needs $\beta$ to fall by a factor of four. The half-life is about $\ln 2/\kappa$ for small $\kappa$, so it grows by a factor of four. At Lesson 1's values the half-life is $37.8$ trials and the standard deviation $0.1005$.

(b) After a jump, the error in $d_p$ decays by a factor $(1 - \kappa)$ per trial, so a small $\beta$ follows slowly. Once it has followed, a small $\beta$ fluctuates less. The right $\beta$ depends on how often the world changes compared with how noisy each trial is. A learner that does not know the rate of change has to learn it, which is a parameter of a model of how the parameters themselves drift. That is a model one level up, the kind of hierarchy Week 5 begins to build.

Exercise 3.5.6Implement it

Using the NumPy notebook's run function, repeat Lesson 3's experiment in a world quieter than the model, $\theta^{*} = \tfrac12$, with learning off and then on. Report the averages of the two errors and of the learning signal over the last 200 trials, and compare their signs with the louder world.

Solution

With learning off, the averages over the last 200 trials are $0.127$ for the prior error, $-0.946$ for the sensory error and $-0.0481$ for the learning signal. With learning on, the power falls from $1$ to $0.880$ by the last trial.

Only the learning signal changes sign between the two worlds. In the louder world the prior error was $0.646$ and the sensory error $-0.035$; here both keep their signs and only their sizes move. A reader who judged the link by the sign of either error alone would call both worlds the same.

Exercise 3.5.7The location in the linear world

In the linear world of Lesson 1, the process draws $d$ from $\mathcal N(d_p^{*}, \Sigma_p^{*})$ on the whole line and $u = d + z$ with $z \sim \mathcal N(0, \Sigma_u^{*})$. The animal holds fixed positive variances $\Sigma_p$ and $\Sigma_u$, not necessarily the world's, and learns $d_p$ by the point rule. Find the drift, show that its only zero is $d_p^{*}$ and that it attracts, and say which step fails in Week 2's model.

Solution

From Lesson 1, the step per unit rate at the mode is $(\hat d - d_p)/\Sigma_p = (u - d_p)/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$. Its average over the process uses $\mathbb E_{P^*}[u] = d_p^{*} + \mathbb E_{P^*}[z] = d_p^{*}$:

$$\varphi(d_p) \;=\; \frac{d_p^{*} - d_p}{\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u} .$$

It vanishes only at $d_p = d_p^{*}$, and $\varphi' = -1/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u) < 0$, so the zero attracts. Neither the animal's variances nor the world's enter the location of the zero, so the location is learned without bias even by an animal whose variances are wrong.

The step that fails in Week 2's model is the first. The mode is not linear in $u$ there, so its average over the process is not the average of the posterior means, and by Lesson 4 the point rule's drift differs from the posterior-averaged rule's by the average of $(m - \hat d)/\Sigma_p$, which is not zero when the posteriors are skewed.

Exercise 3.5.8The two pulls on the source power

For the source power, the per-trial derivative as a function of the state is $\psi(d) = \big(u - \theta h(d)\big)h(d)/\Sigma_u$ with $h(d) = 1/d^2$. Find $\psi'$ and $\psi''$, evaluate both where the residual $u - \theta h(d)$ is zero, and say what the second-order expansion of Lesson 4 can and cannot tell you about the sign of the point rule's error in $\theta$.

Solution

Differentiating the product,

$$\psi' \;=\; \frac{h'\,(u - 2\theta h)}{\Sigma_u}, \qquad \psi'' \;=\; \frac{(u - 2\theta h)\,h'' - 2\theta h'^2}{\Sigma_u} .$$

With $h = d^{-2}$, $h' = -2d^{-3}$ and $h'' = 6d^{-4}$. Where $u = \theta h$, the factor $u - 2\theta h$ is $-\theta h = -\theta d^{-2}$, so

$$\psi' \;=\; \frac{2\theta}{\Sigma_u d^{5}} \;>\; 0, \qquad \psi'' \;=\; \frac{-6\theta - 8\theta}{\Sigma_u d^{6}} \;=\; -\frac{14\theta}{\Sigma_u d^{6}} \;<\; 0 .$$

In the expansion $\mathbb E_{d\mid u}[\psi] - \psi(\hat d) \approx \psi'(m - \hat d) + \tfrac12 \psi''[V + (m - \hat d)^2]$, a posterior whose mean lies beyond its mode makes the first term positive, and the posterior variance makes the second negative. The posterior-averaged rule has zero drift at the truth, so the point rule's drift there is minus the average of the exact gap. The expansion shows two contributions of opposite sign, so the error can take either sign, but it does not say which wins: $\psi$ is not a polynomial, and the terms it drops matter. In Lesson 3's louder world the second-order sum averages to $-0.7429$ while the exact gap averages to $0.0303$. The sign comes from the full posterior calculation, which gives the power overestimated when the world equals Week 2's model and underestimated in the louder world.

Exercise 3.5.9Learning with momentum †

Buckley et al. (2017), section 8, write the dynamics of a parameter in second-order form: the parameter's acceleration, rather than its velocity, is proportional to the gradient. Apply this to the prior location in the linear model, in continuous time and with the update replaced by its expectation over trials.

(a) Show that the drift is $(\bar u - d_p)/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$, where $\bar u$ is the mean of $u$ over the process, and write the second-order equation for $d_p$.

(b) Solve it from rest at $d_p(0) \neq \bar u$ and describe the motion.

(c) Add a friction term $-b\,\dot d_p$ and find the value of $b$ that returns $d_p$ to $\bar u$ fastest without overshoot.

Solution

(a) In the linear model $\hat d - d_p = \Sigma_p(u - d_p)/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$, so the gradient $-\varepsilon_p = (\hat d - d_p)/\Sigma_p = (u - d_p)/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$, with no truncation term on the whole line. Its average over the process is $(\bar u - d_p)/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$. With a rate constant $\beta$,

$$\ddot d_p \;=\; \frac{\beta}{\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u}\,(\bar u - d_p) .$$

(b) Write $x = d_p - \bar u$ and $\omega_0^2 = \beta/(\Sigma_p + \Sigma_u)$. Then $\ddot x = -\omega_0^2 x$, with solution $x(t) = x(0)\cos\omega_0 t$ from rest. The prior location oscillates about the target forever with constant amplitude, passing through $\bar u$ at full speed and reaching $\bar u - x(0)$ on the other side. Gradient flow in first-order form, $\dot x = -\omega_0^2 x$, decays to the target instead. The second-order form needs something else to stop.

(c) With friction, $\ddot x + b\dot x + \omega_0^2 x = 0$, whose characteristic roots are $\tfrac12\big(-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4\omega_0^2}\big)$. For $b < 2\omega_0$ the roots are complex and $x$ oscillates with decaying amplitude; for $b > 2\omega_0$ both are real and negative and the slower one, $\tfrac12\big(-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4\omega_0^2}\big)$, approaches zero as $b$ grows, so heavy friction is slow. The fastest return without overshoot is at $b = 2\omega_0$, critical damping, where $x(t) = x(0)(1 + \omega_0 t)e^{-\omega_0 t}$.

Exercise 3.5.10Precision as the learned quantity in the circuit

Week 2's circuit applies each precision as a gain at an error unit. Suppose the animal stores $\Pi_u$ at that unit and learns it in log coordinates. Write the update, say what it uses, and check that its stationary point is the same as that of the log-variance update.

Solution

Since $\ln\Pi_u = -\ln\Sigma_u$, the derivative in $\ln\Pi_u$ is minus the derivative in $\ln\Sigma_u$, which Lesson 2 found to be $\tfrac12(\delta_u^2/\Sigma_u - 1)$. So

$$\Pi_u \;\leftarrow\; \Pi_u\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big(1 - \Pi_u\delta_u^2\big)\Big),$$

and since $\Pi_u\delta_u^2 = \varepsilon_u\delta_u$, it uses the error unit's activity, the raw residual and the gain itself. The drift in $\ln\Pi_u$ vanishes where $\mathbb E_{P^*}[\Pi_u\delta_u^2] = 1$, that is $\mathbb E_{P^*}[\delta_u^2] = \Sigma_u$, the same zero as the log-variance update, since a step in one coordinate is exactly the opposite step in the other. That zero is where the average change in the logarithm vanishes. The average change in $\Pi_u$ itself is not zero there: the step multiplies $\Pi_u$ by an exponential, whose average exceeds one whenever the exponent varies, as Lesson 2 noted for the variance.
